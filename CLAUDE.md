# Swing Out Delhi — Claude Project Context

## 1. Project purpose

You are helping build and maintain the official website for **Swing Out Delhi**, a growing Lindy Hop and swing dance community in Delhi, India.

This is a community-led cultural project, not a generic commercial dance academy.

The website should make a visitor feel:

> **I could belong here.**

The central brand line is:

> **Find your rhythm. Find your people.**

A second recurring phrase is:

> **Would you like to dance?**

The website should communicate the joy, warmth, music, movement, friendship, curiosity and international connections around Swing Out Delhi.

---

## 2. Brand story

Swing Out Delhi was founded by **Shivi Chandna** after she returned to Lindy Hop following a long break from dancing.

Shivi first discovered Lindy Hop in Cambridge, UK in 2011 and danced actively from 2011–2016. During that period she trained regularly, taught beginner classes, volunteered in the Cambridge swing community, helped organise the Cambridge Lindy Exchange, attended workshops with international teachers and danced across swing scenes in the UK, Europe, Japan and China.

An ankle injury in 2016 forced her to stop dancing. She later returned to India and spent several years dealing with the injury and rehabilitation.

After finally being able to dance again, she looked for a Lindy Hop community in Delhi and found that there was no established active scene.

Swing Out Delhi grew from the desire to bring Lindy Hop back into her life and to share the joy of the dance with others.

The community is now growing through classes, socials, free practice sessions, jazz events, collaborations, visiting dancers and teachers, international connections and community activities.

## 6. International connection

International exchange is an important part of Swing Out Delhi's identity.

The community has welcomed dancers from different countries and Indian cities.

Swing Out Delhi also encourages its dancers to travel and connect with international swing communities.

A number of community dancers are travelling to **Thailand Swing Era in 2026**, demonstrating that the Delhi scene is becoming connected to the wider global Lindy Hop community.

Shivi was awarded the **Frankie Manning Foundation scholarship** for Thailand Swing Era, which is an important milestone in the community's development.

Do not overstate this or turn the website into a personal achievement page. It should support the broader story of international exchange and community growth.

## 9. Visual identity

The attached Swing Out Delhi logo is the primary visual reference.

Key visual characteristics:

- deep navy blue
- mustard/golden yellow
- warm cream
- vintage script lettering
- dancing silhouettes
- musical/jazz references

Primary palette should be approximately:

- Deep navy: #0B2742 / similar
- Mustard/gold: #F2C43D / similar
- Warm cream: #F4F0E5 / similar

Use the actual logo colours where possible rather than inventing a completely new palette.

### Typography

The logo uses a distinctive vintage script style.

Use a similar expressive script/display treatment for major headings when appropriate.

However:

- body text must remain highly readable
- navigation must be clean
- don't use script everywhere
- don't sacrifice accessibility for aesthetics

The overall typographic feeling should be like a modern interpretation of a vintage jazz poster.

## 11. Tone of voice

Write like a real person.

The tone is:

- conversational
- warm
- concise
- friendly
- slightly playful
- confident without being corporate

Avoid AI/corporate clichés such as:

- "empowering individuals"
- "fostering transformative experiences"
- "unlock your potential"
- "like-minded individuals"
- "creating synergies"
- "journey of self-discovery"
- "passionate community"
- "holistic experience"

Prefer simple language.

Examples:

> Never danced before? Perfect.

> No partner? No problem.

> Come for the dancing. Stay for the people.

> Would you like to dance?

> Find your rhythm. Find your people.

> There's always room on the dance floor.

Do not overuse exclamation marks.

Do not make every sentence sound like marketing copy.

2. Make beginners feel comfortable.

4. Showcase the community.

6. Show Delhi's connection to the global swing community.
7. Encourage people to join the community.
8. Encourage visiting dancers and teachers to connect.
9. Showcase merchandise.

- native class registration
- event RSVPs
- payments
- newsletter
- member database
- teacher profiles
- event calendar

- community directory

Do not build those advanced systems unless explicitly requested.

## 16. Design behaviour

Use subtle motion only.

Good:
- gentle image reveals
- hover states
- subtle scrolling movement
- tasteful marquee/event ticker
- small interactions inspired by rhythm

Avoid:
- excessive animations
- distracting parallax
- autoplay audio
- unnecessary loading effects
- gimmicky animations

The site should feel alive, not chaotic.

---

## 17. Technical principles

Use a clean, maintainable architecture.

Prioritise:

- semantic HTML
- accessibility
- responsive design
- SEO
- fast loading
- optimised images
- reusable components
- clear data structures
- easy event updates

Keep registration and external links configurable.

Do not introduce unnecessary dependencies.

Do not over-engineer the first version.

Before adding a library, consider whether the same result can be achieved cleanly without it.

---

## 18. Development workflow

Before major implementation:

1. Inspect all supplied assets.
2. Identify the strongest photos.
3. Analyse the logo.
4. Establish the visual system.
5. Create the homepage first.
6. Run it locally.
7. Check mobile and desktop.
8. Review visual hierarchy.
9. Then build secondary pages.

When making changes:

- preserve working functionality
- don't rewrite unrelated sections
- don't replace real photos with placeholders
- don't change the brand palette without a reason
- don't make the design more generic

If something feels like a generic dance-school website, rethink it.

## 19. Creative north star

Imagine walking into a Swing Out Delhi evening at Depot 48.

Jazz is playing.

Some people are experienced dancers.

Some have just learnt their first few steps.

People are laughing.

Someone is practising a move with a friend.

Someone has come alone and has already found someone to dance with.

A visiting dancer from another country is on the floor.

There is music, movement, conversation and community.

There may even be cake.

The website should capture that feeling.

The goal is not simply to say:

> "We offer dance classes."

The goal is to communicate:

> **There is a community here. Come be part of it.**
